## 2. Consumer behaviour (why reviews matter)

- BrightLocal's _Local Consumer Review Survey 2026_ is titled "Star Ratings Keep Rising, Old Reviews Don't Cut It". Recency is a key purchase factor [V-2] ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/consumer-review-survey/), [PinMeTo summary](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/brightlocal-local-consumer-review-survey-2026/)).
- Secondary sources quote "89% of consumers expect businesses to respond to reviews" and similar figures. **These are not verified at source [V-2]. Do not use them in marketing until confirmed from the BrightLocal report itself.**

Implication: the _collection_ of fresh reviews is as commercially important as _responding_ to them.

### 3.4 The platform threat: Google itself

Search Engine Land reported (2026) that **Google Business Profile is testing AI-generated replies to reviews**. Merchants review, edit and submit them inside GBP, mainly in the US, Brazil and India [V-2] ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-business-profile-test-reply-to-reviews-with-ai-472167)).

**Implication:** a free, native "suggest a reply" will reach every owner. ReviewPilot cannot charge for that. It **can** charge for:

- multi-location governance, approval and audit;
- brand voice consistency at scale;
- risk-aware automation;
- cross-location intelligence and issue tracking;
- review collection;
- reporting for owners and franchisors.

Google's native tool will be per-profile and generic.

## 4. Gaps and opportunities

1. **Price gap:** enterprise suites cost $150–$450+ per location; commodity tools cost €0–30. There is an underserved **€40–90 per location** band for EU hospitality groups that need governance and intelligence, not a full CX suite. [A, based on V-2 pricing]
2. **Localisation gap:** Dutch-first UI, Dutch-quality AI replies, NL/EU data hosting, Dutch DPA (_verwerkersovereenkomst_), iDEAL/SEPA billing.
3. **Operations gap:** most tools stop at a sentiment chart. Converting complaints into assigned and measured issues is rare at SMB price points [A, validate in demos].
4. **Hospitality specificity:** dish- and menu-level sentiment, service vs kitchen vs ambience topics, shift and day-part patterns (with caveats), delivery vs dine-in distinction.

## 5. Is "independent restaurants first" attractive? — **No, not as the primary beachhead.**

| Factor                     | Independents                                          | Groups (3–30 locations)                 |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Willingness to pay         | Low. Many will choose Google's free AI or no tool [A] | Medium–high (governance pain)           |
| Review volume per location | Often low (5–30/mo) [A], so little time saved         | Aggregated volume is high               |
| Churn                      | High. Sector bankruptcies peaked in 2025 [V]          | Lower. Embedded in operations [A]       |
| Sales effort per €         | High (many small deals)                               | Better (one deal = N locations)         |
| Value of intelligence      | Weak (small sample sizes)                             | Strong (cross-location comparison)      |
| Decision maker             | Owner, time-poor                                      | Ops or marketing manager with a mandate |

**Recommendation:** lead with groups and franchises. Serve independents through a **self-serve Essentials tier** with zero sales touch, which also feeds word-of-mouth. Revisit independents as a volume segment once acquisition via partners (POS, reservation) proves cheap.

## 6. Market entry strategy: the Netherlands

1. **Design partners (months 4–6):** 5–10 groups with 3–30 locations, discounted for feedback and case studies. Source them from the founders' network, KHN events, and LinkedIn outreach to ops managers.
2. **Category content in Dutch:** "reageren op Google reviews", reply templates, a free tool (e.g. a "review health check" for a single location using the Places API, within its terms). Use it for SEO and lead generation.
3. **Partnerships:** POS and reservation vendors (integration and referral), hospitality marketing agencies (reseller margin of 20–30%), franchise consultants.
4. **Events:** Horecava (January, Amsterdam) and regional KHN meetings.
5. **Expansion order:** NL → Flanders (BE) → DACH (German) → Nordics/FR, depending on partner traction.
